## Abstract 
 The current research proposes a full machine learning framework that would forecast the presence of a rainbow in 34 state capitals and union territories in India with the help of the high-resolution meteorological data. NASA POWER API has been used to assemble over 1.49 million hourly measurements of five years of observation (2020-2024) of ten atmospheric variables such as solar irradiance, precipitation, humidity and solar zenith angle. To enhance predictive capability, 26 additional features were created, including the parameters of physics (solar altitude and solar azimuth), and a new composite measure called the Rainbow Score (the value was between 0 and 10), which was created to assess the atmospheric conditions favourable to rainbow formation.
 In order to generate ground truth data, ground truth was generated with the help of a physics-informed labeling methodology and these four supervised machine learning models were trained and tested: XGBoost, Random Forest, LightGBM and Logistic Regression. Among them, XGBoost has been demonstrated to be nearly perfect with the accuracy, F1-score and ROC-AUC of 1.00, which can be attributed to the power of the physical modeling behind it. In addition, K-Means clustering (k=2) unsupervised learning revealed two statistically distinct meteorological regimes that are supported by a Silhouette Score of 0.471 and significant ANOVA test (p < 0.05). As a result of the analysis, the following critical conditions to existence of the rainbow were identified: the altitude of the sun, Monsoon seasonality, and some times of the day (early morning and late afternoon).
 This paper is the first substantial machine-based learning study of the prediction of rainbows in the Indian subcontinent and it provides a platform which can be extended to explore the atmospheric optical phenomenon.
